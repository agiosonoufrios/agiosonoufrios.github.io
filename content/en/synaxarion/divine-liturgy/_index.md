## The Divine Liturgy and Its Parts

The Divine Liturgy is the centre of Orthodox worship. It is the greatest mystery of our Church, the mystery of the presence of Christ among us. For this reason it always remains the only hope of true life for man. The word *Liturgy* (*leitourgia*) is ancient and meant any work done for the people. The holy Liturgy has three parts:

1. The first is the ***Proskomide***.
2. The second is the ***Liturgy of the Catechumens***, because in this part the Catechumens, too, remain at the Liturgy.
3. The third is called the ***Liturgy of the Faithful***, because in this part only the Faithful remain at the Liturgy, while the others leave the church.

## Holy Communion

Faith in the Mystery of Holy Communion, the faith that in the Holy Chalice is present bodily the God-man Jesus Christ Himself, the faith, more particularly, that Holy Communion is food most necessary for our spiritual existence: this faith gives birth in the soul of the believer to the longing and yearning to come into direct contact, to become one with our God-man Lord. In Holy Communion we do not merely meet Jesus Christ; we become one with Him. We become of one body and one blood with Christ. We become Christ-bearers. Christ, speaking once to the Jews, said: “Unless you eat My flesh and drink My blood, you have no life in you.”[^iw6a] They, because they did not understand the spiritual meaning of His words, were scandalised and went back, for they thought that the Saviour of the world was urging them to eat human flesh.[^iw6b] Do not think, then, that the Bread and the Wine are simply bread and wine. But, as the Master Christ Himself has clearly declared, they are His Body and Blood. And even if the sense of taste makes you perceive them otherwise, let faith assure you of the truth. Do not judge the matter by taste, but be fully assured by faith that you have truly been counted worthy to receive the Body and Blood of Christ.

The Holy Fathers write that the water and the blood are the image of holy Baptism and of the divine Eucharist; these two Mysteries, flowing from the side of Christ, create the Church. Christ, then, created the Church from His side, just as He created Eve from the side of Adam. And just as she was created while Adam slept, so the Church was created during the sleep of the new Adam.[^chrys30] A divine-human encounter, a communion across the ages, the genuine seal of Apostolic succession, a foretaste of paradise, the heart of Orthodox mystagogy, the extension of loving communion and of the personal “inner room” of prayer: such is the Church, with the Holy Table at its centre. The Service, a miniature of the heavenly Liturgy, becomes at times supplicatory and at times doxological, epic and vigorous. At moments the lights of the church go out and there is silence, a symbol of the Burial of Christ; at other times the chanting swells, the chandeliers swing and the choirs are all aglow. Sacred and immaculate moments, communications of the Heavenly Gladness.

## The Holy Eucharist, Food of the New Life

In order to live the new, reborn life as a member of the Church, man is nourished with new and heavenly food, which in the language of the Church is called the divine Eucharist. The divine Eucharist is the central liturgical act (sacred mystery) by which the spiritual organism of the Church is nourished and given life. The celebration and distribution of the mystery take place in a special service of the Church called the Divine Liturgy. The divine Liturgy is the place of worship of the faithful, who touch the Inaccessible and Invisible through the experience of the soul and of the prayer of the heart. The distribution of the mystery is called Holy Communion or divine Metalepsis. Communion at the Divine Liturgy is the antidote to the impasses of modern man: anxiety, insecurity, emptiness, uncertainty and loneliness.

The consecration of the gifts, that is, the sacrifice itself, proclaims His death, resurrection and ascension, as these gifts are changed into the very body of the Lord, the body that was crucified, rose and ascended. What precedes the sacrifice manifests the events that took place before the death of the Lord, that is, His coming into the world, His public appearance, His miracles and His teaching. And what follows the sacrifice symbolises the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the Apostles, the return of mankind to God and their communion with Him.

According to the holy Augustine, the creation and the Sabbath rest have a deeper meaning for the re-creation of the world by the Son of God. That is, just as the Father, having created the world, rested, so too the Son of God, having laboured for the redemption of the human race, rested in the tomb, having said on the Cross: “It is finished!” For us Christians, the place of the Sabbath is taken not by the seventh day but by the first day of the week, Sunday. On this day, through the resurrection of Christ, and sealed by the descent of the Holy Spirit fifty days later, the work of Redemption was accomplished: the new creation, the re-creation, far higher, more wondrous and more miraculous than the first creation, leading us to the blessed Sabbath, eternal and blessed rest. That Sabbath was the type; this Sunday is the reality. Sunday is the birthday of the salvation and re-creation of man. According to Saint Gregory the Theologian, “just as the first creation began on Sunday, so the second creation also began again from Sunday.”[^greg44] This new Creation of God, says Saint Gregory, is more radiant and more wondrous than the radiant and wondrous first Creation, “because it leads to the heavenly state”. For this reason the means of sanctification of this new Sabbath, Sunday, which we Christians possess in order to attain eternal rest in heaven, are higher than the Hebrew Sabbath observance. When the Lord says, “My Father has been working until now, and I have been working,”[^iw5] He means the divine preservation and governance of the world, without which the world would be reduced to nothing.

## Unity with One’s Brother and with God

The purpose of the Divine Liturgy is precisely to lead the faithful into a living relationship and unity with their brother and with God. In the Church “we enjoy one another”, because we all enjoy the common “banquet of faith”.[^chryskat] At the end of the Divine Liturgy the common Chalice will reveal that “the multitude of those who believed were of one heart and one soul; neither did any one of them say that any of the things he possessed was his own, but they had all things in common.”[^praxeis] Orthodox Christians gather, above all, every Sunday in their parish church, where all together, headed by the Priest, they celebrate the Divine Liturgy. The Divine Liturgy is not simply a public and common prayer, as for example that of Muslims, but a realisation and manifestation of the Kingdom of God in the time and space of this world.

## Prefigurations of the Cross

The sacrifice of Christ on the Cross was prophetically prefigured by Moses when he hung the bronze serpent on the wood, so that whoever was bitten by a living serpent (which itself prefigured sin and death through sin), looking upon the bronze serpent, might be saved, thereby showing his faith in God and in His Prophet.[^arith21] If, then, a bronze serpent crucified saves, can the Son of God, God who also became man, Crucified, not save? From ancient times until today human life has been sustained and protected in relation to wood. In the time of Noah, as is well known, the life of the righteous was saved by the wooden ark. Again, in the time of Moses the sea, as soon as it saw the wooden rod, which also prefigured the Cross of Christ, feared him who touched it. Does the rod of Moses, then, have power, and the Cross of the Saviour lack it? Let us remember that the wood in the time of Moses sweetened the water,[^ex15] and “that from the side of Jesus the water flowed upon the wood”.[^iw19]

## The Lord’s Day

Of the seven days of the week, God gave us six to work and one for prayer and rest, for the forgiveness of our sins. That is why it was named *Kyriaki*, the Lord’s Day, because it is the sovereign (*kyria*) of the days. For before the Passion of the Lord it was not called Kyriaki but the first day. On this day the Lord began the Resurrection and gave the commandment that the Holy Mysteries be celebrated. Sunday, then, became the beginning of every good for us: the beginning of the creation of the world, the beginning of the Resurrection, the beginning of the week. This day has three beginnings, signifying the authority of the all-holy Trinity. According to Basil the Great, Sunday is now an image of the age to come, and afterwards it will be the age to come itself.[^basil27] Taking part in the divine Liturgy, especially on Sunday, our mind is carried from earthly things to heavenly ones, to the unwaning day of the Kingdom of the Lord. Celebrating in every divine Liturgy the Pascha of the Lord, we praise Christ: “O great and most sacred Pascha, Christ! O Wisdom, Word and Power of God! Grant us to partake of Thee more fully (that is, more perfectly and purely) in the unwaning day of Thy Kingdom.”[^pascha]

The faithful who attend church and take part in all this with attentive mind become firmer in faith and more fervent in piety and in their love for God. The work of the Divine Liturgy is the change of the gifts offered by the faithful, the bread and the wine, into the body and blood of Christ. And its purpose is the sanctification of the faithful, who through Holy Communion receive the forgiveness of their sins, the inheritance of the kingdom of heaven and every spiritual good.

## The Mystery of the Holy Eucharist

The mystery of the divine Eucharist was instituted by God. The divine Eucharist is both mystery and sacrifice, and indeed a holy, mystical and rational offering. Through the divine Eucharist the faithful partake of the true body and blood of Christ and are united with Him mystically, becoming “partakers of the divine nature”.[^2pet1] The divine Eucharist was instituted by the Lord Himself as the eternal mystery of the New Covenant. The mystery of the divine Eucharist was instituted by the Lord on the evening of Holy Thursday. That is, one evening, before He was betrayed, He called His disciples to the Mystical Supper and commanded them and their successors to do the same in remembrance of Him. The evening was a sign that the fullness of time had come and that things were reaching their end. And our Lord gave thanks at this supper to teach us how we must celebrate the mystery, to show that He was not going to His Passion against His will, and that we must patiently and thankfully endure whatever we suffer, thus strengthening our hopes. For if the typical Passover freed the Jews from such harsh slavery, how much more does the true Pascha free the whole world and is given for the benefit of our sinful nature. For this reason He did not hand down the mystery before the Passover, but handed it down when the prefigurations of the Law were to cease. We have the body and blood of our Christ, which imparts to us life and incorruption. The fruits of the mystery of Holy Communion are the forgiveness of sins, the sanctification of soul and body, the theosis of the faithful and the union of all Christians who commune in one body. Just as material food is necessary for the life and growth of man, so too Holy Communion, as divine food, is necessary for the preservation and growth of the Christian in the reborn life.

The Holy Proskomide is so called from the verb *proskomizo*, “to bring forward”, because in it the bread and wine are brought forward, that is, offered to God the King of all, in remembrance of Christ, who offered Himself as a sacrifice to God the Father through the offering of His Body, as the sacrificed Lamb and as High Priest and Son of Man, offering and offered, and at the same time sacrificed. And according to Patriarch Jeremias of Constantinople, “just as Jesus incarnate was dedicated to God from the beginning and was a precious gift to Him” from the very moment He was conceived in the most pure womb of the Ever-Virgin Theotokos, so now, as soon as the order of the Mystery begins, a spotless Sacrifice is offered to God Most High through the precious Gifts. The bread and wine which the faithful offer for the Liturgy, and which symbolise the body and blood of the Lord, are not placed from the beginning on the Altar for the sacrifice, but are first placed on the holy Prothesis and dedicated to God as precious gifts; this is henceforth their name.

## The Holy Prothesis

The niche of the holy Prothesis, which symbolises the holy cave of the Nativity of the Lord, is located on the north-east side of the sanctuary. Here the precious Gifts are prepared before they are transferred at the Great Entrance to the holy Table to be consecrated. This niche bears the iconographic representation of the Extreme Humility (*Akra Tapeinosis*) and, rarely, the icon of the Nativity.

Patriarch Germanus of Constantinople says the following about the holy Prothesis: “The divine Body is placed there as in Bethlehem, where Christ was born, and at the same time as in Nazareth. The Prothesis also fulfils the type of His sojourn in Capernaum, and of the place of Golgotha.”[^germanos] Nazareth is the city of Galilee where the Annunciation was made to the Theotokos. It is very well known as the home town of the Theotokos and of Joseph and as the city in which Jesus Christ lived before He began His teaching work. Jesus grew up in Nazareth after His return from Egypt, while Capernaum is the city of Galilee near the Lake of Gennesaret, whose Hebrew name is Kfar Nahum, that is, “village of consolations” or “village of Nahum”. In the Gospels Capernaum is mentioned as the second dwelling place of the Lord after Nazareth, and as the home of the apostles Peter, Andrew, James, John and Matthew. Our Lord Jesus Christ taught in the synagogue of Capernaum and performed many well-known healings in that city. In Capernaum Christ proclaimed Himself the bread of life, of which those who eat shall never hunger. In Capernaum our Lord proclaims:

> I am the bread of life. Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead. This is the bread which comes down from heaven, that one may eat of it and not die. I am the living bread which came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live for ever; and the bread that I shall give is My flesh, which I shall give for the life of the world.[^iw6c]

On the holy Prothesis lie the spoon (*labis*), with which the faithful receive Communion; the holy lance, which symbolises the lance that pierced the Lord at the crucifixion; the sponge, which also symbolises the sponge with which vinegar was given to the Lord; and the *Zeon*, a vessel containing hot water, which is poured into the holy Chalice and symbolises the fervour of faith. There are also the veils of the holy vessels, the *aer* and the sacred cloth. Finally, on the holy Prothesis there is also the holy Chalice, in which the wine and the water are mixed, which will be changed into the Blood of Christ. Beside it is the holy Diskarion (paten), on which is placed the Lamb, the portion of bread that will be changed into the Body of Christ, as well as other portions from the prosphoron, dedicated to the Theotokos, the holy angels, the living faithful and the faithful departed. This sacred whole upon the paten symbolises the unity of the Church. Upon the holy paten there is also the Asteriskos (star-cover), which symbolises the star of the Nativity and serves to keep the holy veil from touching the portions of the holy Bread.

## The Sacrifice of Melchizedek

A type of the Mystery of the Holy Eucharist was also the sacrifice of Melchizedek, who was a Priest of God Most High and offered in his sacrifices nothing other than bread and wine.

> And the king of Sodom went out to meet him, after he returned from the slaughter of Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him, into the valley of Shaveh (that is, the King’s Valley). And Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was the priest of God Most High. And he blessed Abram and said: “Blessed be Abram of God Most High, who made heaven and earth; and blessed be God Most High, who has delivered your enemies into your hand.” And Abram gave him a tithe of all. And the king of Sodom said to Abram, “Give me the men, and take the horses for yourself.”[^gen14]

Καὶ Μελχισεδὲκ βασιλεὺς Σαλὴμ ἐξήνεγκεν ἄρτους καὶ οἶνον· ἦν δὲ ἱερεὺς τοῦ Θεοῦ τοῦ ὑψίστου. Καὶ εὐλόγησε τὸν Ἄβραμ καὶ εἶπεν· εὐλογημένος Ἄβραμ τῷ Θεῷ τῷ ὑψίστῳ, ὃς ἔκτισε τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν. Καὶ εὐλογητὸς ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ὕψιστος, ὃς παρέδωκε τοὺς ἐχθρούς σου ὑποχειρίους σοι. Καὶ ἔδωκεν αὐτῷ Ἄβραμ δεκάτην ἀπὸ πάντων. Εἶπε δὲ βασιλεὺς Σοδόμων πρὸς Ἄβραμ· δός μοι τοὺς ἄνδρας, τὴν δὲ ἵππον λάβε σεαυτῷ.</p></details>

According to the Fathers of our Church, Melchizedek offers bread and wine so that a sacrifice may be offered for the victory of Abraham. This follows from the phrase “he was the priest of God Most High”. Consequently the bread and wine offered by Melchizedek are a prefiguration of the bloodless sacrifice of Christ, the divine Eucharist. We offer God bread and wine because they are exclusively human food, by which our life is sustained and expressed. For this reason it is believed that when one offers food, it is as if one offers life itself. Since, then, through the divine Mysteries God grants us eternal life, it was natural that our own gift should also, in some way, be life, so that our offering would not be out of keeping with God’s recompense, but have something akin to it.

Irenaeus, in the second century after Christ, repeatedly speaks of the mystery of the Eucharist and points out that “the mixed cup and the manufactured bread receive the Word of God, and the Eucharist becomes the body of Christ and the blood of Christ”.[^eirinaios] Thus the Eucharist is the new sacrifice and offering which the Church, having received it from the Apostles, offers to God throughout the whole world. Just as from many grains of wheat one bread is made, and wine from various grapes, so from many Faithful one body of the Catholic Holy Church is made, united with the Master Christ Himself, who is the Head of the Church. Thus the Lord commanded us to offer Him bread and wine, and He in return gives us “heavenly bread” and the “cup of life”. He willed that we should offer Him provisions of this transient life, and that He should offer us in return eternal life, so that His Grace might appear as a reward and His immeasurable mercy as an act of justice.

## The Three Ends of the Eucharistic Sacrifice

The ends of the Eucharistic Sacrifice are three: the latreutic (worship), the propitiatory and the eucharistic (thanksgiving). The latreutic belongs to the Holy Bread, because it alone is offered in remembrance and worship of the Son of God. The propitiatory refers to the portions of sinners, because through them, too, the great God, who is angered by our sins, is propitiated and appeased. The eucharistic belongs to the Portions of the Saints, because through them God is thanked and glorified for the many benefits He has granted them, among which is that they intercede with Him for our salvation. After the completion of the Proskomide, the celebrant covers the precious gifts with the veils and censes the holy Prothesis and the holy Table, thus signifying the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the world and upon the gifts offered.

The Divine Liturgy is also called the Divine Eucharist (Thanksgiving), because we thank God “for all that He has done and does for us” and at the same time offer Him our rational worship as a eucharistic gift, having nothing else of our own to give Him, since even this life of ours does not belong to us; it is His offering, His benefaction.

## The Order of the Holy Proskomide

We distinguish four points in the Holy Proskomide: a) the preparation of the clergy, b) the preparation of the Lamb and the chalice, c) the commemorations and the censing, and finally d) the dismissal. At the Holy Proskomide the Priest, having said the “Blessed is…”, venerates together with the Deacon the holy Icons of the Lord and enters the holy Sanctuary, making three prostrations towards the East in worship of God, praised in Trinity, the God of all. He puts on the priestly vestments and, going before the Prothesis, washes his hands, signifying his purity and blamelessness in the sacred rite (as Symeon of Thessalonica interprets), and that one must approach the Pure without any stain, as far as is possible for man. He then takes the so-called *Prosphora* and, blessing in the form of a cross over the Seal (the *prosphoridion*, as it is called), says: “In remembrance of our Lord and God and Saviour Jesus Christ.” Then, with the Holy Lance, the Priest cuts the four sides of the Seal and pronounces the prophetic words of Isaiah: “He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and as a lamb before its shearer is silent, so He opens not His mouth.”[^is53] This is done, first, in remembrance of the sacrifice of the Son of God on the Cross; second, for the sanctification and blessing of the Precious Bread; and third, so that at the time of Holy Communion the *Margarites* (Pearl) may easily be divided in four (the Precious Bread and all the parts into which it is divided are called the Pearl).

Immediately afterwards the Deacon, as a type of the Archangel Gabriel who said “Rejoice” to the holy Virgin, takes the Seal out of the middle of the Prosphoron, as happened in Bethlehem at the Birth of Christ from the most pure womb of the Theotokos. And because the all-holy Virgin stood on the right side of the life-giving cross at the Crucifixion of the Lord, a Portion is taken out in her honour and memory and placed to the right of the Precious Bread. Portions are also taken out for all the Saints, for their glory, honour and sanctification. Then the portions of the other Christians, living and departed, are taken out, and separately, by name, those of the persons who offered these gifts, for the forgiveness of sins and for life eternal. The custom of the Portions, and especially those of the Saints, has existed in the Church from very ancient times and is described by the divine Dionysius in his work *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*.

The prosphoron is round to show that God is without beginning and without end. It symbolises the Theotokos, the Virgin Mary. Being brought by the faithful Christian to the holy Church, and by the celebrant to the holy Prothesis, it signifies the journey to Bethlehem for the census. Thus we see that the Service of the Proskomide, which begins with the Elevation of the prosphora and continues with the taking out of the Lamb, symbolises the Birth of Christ up to His going forth to preach. The celebrant takes out the remaining portions, and before the Proskomide is completed, the earthly and heavenly Church is present on the holy Paten: the Lamb exactly in the centre (everywhere and always the Lord must be at the centre), the portion of the Theotokos, the nine Orders of the Saints (Angels, Prophets, Apostles, Hierarchs, Martyrs, Venerable Fathers, Unmercenaries, the Ancestors of God, together with the Saint or Saints of the day, and the Saint whose Divine Liturgy is being celebrated, Chrysostom, Basil or James), the portion of the Bishop, the portions of the living and the portions of the departed (see the [Order of the Divine Liturgy](/en/synaxarion/divine-liturgy/diataxi/) and [On the Celebration of the Holy Proskomide](/en/synaxarion/divine-liturgy/proskomide/)).

## Bread and Wine

The elements used in the mystery of the Holy Eucharist are leavened bread and wine mixed with a moderate quantity of water. The mixing of wine with water in the chalice is done in remembrance of the blood and water that flowed from the pierced side of the Saviour. Father Cyprian (cited by the Council in Trullo) sees symbolically in the mixing of wine and water in the chalice the union of Christ with His Church, and observes that when water is mingled with wine in the chalice, the people are united with Christ; so that if one offers only wine, the blood of Christ is understood to exist without us, and again, when only water is in the chalice, the multitude is understood to exist without Christ. In addition, the Council of Carthage in 397 stresses that, since the predominant element in the consecrated chalice is the fruit of the vine, it follows that the quantity of water mixed with the wine must be such that the nature and the perception of the wine are not substantially altered.

## The Beginning of the Divine Liturgy

The Divine Liturgy begins with the doxology of the Triune God, “Blessed is the Kingdom of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever and unto the ages of ages”, and with the sign of the Cross, which the celebrant makes with the Gospel over the holy Table (over the Antimension). Concerning this opening of the Divine Liturgy, the blessed Germanus, Patriarch of Constantinople, points out that the Divine Liturgy begins with “Blessed is the Kingdom…” because it was through the Incarnation of the Lord that men first learned that God is three persons. The rites celebrated are the mystagogy of this Incarnation of the Lord. Thus, in the prelude of these rites, the Trinity is proclaimed:

> For it was through the Incarnation of the Lord that men first learned that God is three persons. And the rites celebrated are the mystagogy of this Incarnation of the Lord. Therefore, in their prelude, the Trinity had to shine forth and be proclaimed.[^germanos]

<details class="orig"><summary>Greek text</summary><p lang="el">Ὅτι διὰ τῆς Ἐνανθρωπήσεως τοῦ Κυρίου, πρώτως ἔμαθον οἱ ἄνθρωποι, ὡς εἴη τρία πρόσωπα ὁ Θεός. Ταύτης δὲ τῆς Ἐνανθρωπήσεως τοῦ Κυρίου, μυσταγωγία ἐστὶ τὰ τελούμενα. Ὅθεν ἐν τοῖς προοιμίοις αὐτῶν ἔδει προλάμπειν καὶ κηρύττεσθαι τὴν Τριάδα.</p></details>

The sign of the Cross signifies that the Cross is the way and the door of entrance into the Kingdom of God. At this point the people respond to the doxological exclamation of the celebrant by saying “Amen” (that is, “so be it”), which shows that the Divine Liturgy is the work of the whole People, of celebrants and concelebrants alike.
