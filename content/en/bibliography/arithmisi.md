Ecclesiastical texts, liturgical books and older editions mainly use three ways of writing numbers: <strong>Arabic</strong> numerals (1, 2, 3…), <strong>Greek alphabetic</strong> numerals (αʹ, βʹ, γʹ…) and <strong>Roman</strong> numerals (I, II, III…). The table below gives their correspondence.

## How a number is written in Greek numerals

The number is broken down into thousands, hundreds, tens and units, and the corresponding letters are written from the largest to the smallest. At the end comes the <strong>keraia</strong> (ʹ), showing that the letters have numerical value. Thousands are marked with the <strong>lower left keraia</strong> (͵) before the letter. Thus the year 2010 is written <span lang="el" class="grnum">͵βιʹ</span> (͵β = 2000, ι = 10). For the numbers 6, 90 and 900 three archaic letters are used: <strong>stigma</strong> (ϛ = 6, often also written “στʹ”), <strong>koppa</strong> (ϟ = 90) and <strong>sampi</strong> (ϡ = 900).
